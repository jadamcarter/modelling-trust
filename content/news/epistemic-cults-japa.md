"Epistemic Cults", by Jesper Kallestrup, J. Adam Carter and Emma C. Gordon, has been accepted for publication in the *Journal of the American Philosophical Association*.

Social epistemology has studied filter bubbles, echo chambers and epistemic bunkers, but not the cult. The paper gives an account of epistemic cults built from four features that reinforce one another: members defer pre-emptively to a leader whose authority is only perceived, the leader controls their isolation, indoctrination is built into the group's structure, and members take on an extremist mindset. The paper argues that what makes a cult distinctively harmful is that the leader takes over its members' epistemic autonomy. Cults are an extreme case of misplaced trust in a perceived authority, which ties the paper to the network's work on trust and expertise.
