On Sunday 8 November, Nicolas Côté and J. Adam Carter presented "Trust as Willingness to Gamble on the Faithfulness of Others", a paper they've co-authored with Emma C. Gordon, at the University of Stirling. The talk was part of the second Responsible Trusting Without Prejudice workshop (7–8 November 2026), run by a project funded by the Royal Society of Edinburgh and hosted by the Universities of Stirling and Edinburgh. Alan Millar chaired the session.

The paper argues that to trust someone is to be willing to stake something you care about on their doing what they've committed to do, on the basis of that commitment. Whether you place that bet, and how much you put on it, is up to you, as an action is. How far their word moves the odds you'll accept isn't, any more than a belief is. That's how the account explains why trust can look like an action in some cases and like a belief in others, why you can't be bribed into trusting someone, and why trust is worth more than mere reliance.
